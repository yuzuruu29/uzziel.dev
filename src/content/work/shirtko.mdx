ShirtKo sells graphic tees and custom prints out of Calauan, Laguna. The owner was running the whole operation through Messenger chats. The build moves the register into the product: shoppers browse a real catalog, check out with GCash transfer or cash on delivery, and Messenger goes back to being support instead of the point of sale.

The storefront is Next.js on Vercel with Supabase behind it. Shoppers sign in with Google or an email magic link; checkout is auth-gated. The catalog carries 30 live designs — 6 signature pieces plus 24 design groups distilled from 214 gallery images — with search, filters, wishlist hearts, and sizes through 5XL. A Custom Print Studio at `/custom` takes artwork uploads and quote requests.

## The trust boundary

The checkout never trusts the browser. Prices come from the static catalog plus an owner-controlled `product_price_overrides` table; the server reprices every line item inside a service-role `create_order_with_items` function, and affiliate credit only attaches from a validated code. Line items carry the print shop's actual MOD Factory blank SKUs, so the owner's supplier order matches the sale without translation.

Payments stay Filipino-practical. The shopper submits a GCash reference on the order page; ShirtKo verifies and marks it paid — the shopper cannot mark their own order. The admin side collapses that loop into one click, from the order page, the table row, or in bulk, with filtered Excel export, a cmd+K command palette, a CSS-only 30-day revenue chart, and an installable PWA.

## Mobile without a second codebase mentality

The Expo app shares the same Supabase backend and a live `/api/catalog` feed with a bundled snapshot fallback. It ships the full premium kit — brand fonts behind a splash gate, FlashList shop grid, sticky-CTA product pages, bag steppers, an orders timeline with GCash receipt submission — and its vitest suite runs 84 tests green.

The site is live, CI runs checks plus catalog-drift detection on every push, and the remaining work is the owner's: confirm real prices, fill the size chart, swap in a hero photo.
